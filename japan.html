Then there's the culture of respect and precision. Trains run exactly on time. Streets are spotless. People are famously polite and helpful, even when there's a language barrier. It's a place where small details matter — and travelers notice the difference immediately. Nature lovers will find just as much to love: cherry blossoms in spring, fiery red maple leaves in autumn, snow-capped mountains, hot springs tucked into forested valleys, and coastlines that feel worlds away from Tokyo's skyscrapers. And the diversity of experiences is huge. You can hike an ancient pilgrimage trail, relax in a traditional ryokan inn, explore a robot-themed arcade, and visit a 1,000-year-old shrine — all within the same week. Few countries offer that kind of contrast so seamlessly. Whether youre drawn by the food, the history, the nature, or the unique blend of old and new, Japan has a way of leaving a lasting impression on everyone who visits.

Which five cities and locations you should visit in Japan

1. Kyoto - Kyoto is Japan's cultural heart, home to thousands of temples, shrines, and traditional geisha districts that showcase centuries of history. Wander through the iconic Fushimi Inari gates or a serene bamboo grove, and you'll feel transported to old Japan.
2. Tokyo - Tokyo is a dazzling mix of futuristic skyscrapers, quirky pop culture, and hidden traditional pockets like Asakusa's ancient temples. It's a city where you can shop, eat, and explore 24/7 and never run out of things to discover.
3. Hokkaido - Hokkaido offers Japan's wildest, most untouched nature, from powder snow perfect for skiing to lavender fields and volcanic hot springs. It's the place to go for outdoor adventure, fresh seafood, and a slower, nature-focused pace of life.
4. Osaka - Osaka is Japan's food capital, famous for street eats like takoyaki and okonomiyaki served up in a lively, laid-back atmosphere. Its friendly locals and vibrant nightlife make it feel more relaxed and fun than its more formal neighbor, Kyoto.
5. Hiroshima - Hiroshima is a city of resilience and peace, with the Peace Memorial Park offering a moving tribute to its history. Just a short trip away, Miyajima Island's floating torii gate adds a stunning, unforgettable finish to the visit.

Pro tipps
  1. Buy your JR RAIL PASS online before departure - it's much cheaper than getting it in Japan.
  2. Carry cash everywhere, since many restaurants and small shops don't accept cards. The cheapest was to do is at a 7 Eleven.
  3. Download a translation app like GOOGLE TRANSLATEwith offline Japanese support , since not everybody can speak English there.

Beyond big attractions like USJ, Japan is full of small, fun activities worth trying. You could visit a themed café, like the many cat, owl, or Pokémon cafés in Tokyo and Osaka, for a quirky and relaxing break. Trying purikura, Japan's iconic photo booths, with friends is another fun way to snap silly, sticker-covered souvenirs. Local arcades in places like Akihabara are great for claw machines, rhythm games, and retro classics. For something more traditional, a visit to a neighborhood sento or onsen offers a relaxing glimpse into everyday Japanese life. Eating at a kaiten-zushi, or conveyor belt sushi restaurant, makes for an affordable and interactive meal. Renting a kimono or yukata for a day and strolling through historic areas like Kyoto's Gion district is perfect for memorable photos, and if the timing works out, stopping by a local matsuri adds street food, games, and fireworks to the mix.
